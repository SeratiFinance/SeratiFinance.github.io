The fixed parity through which ECB policy reaches 14 African economies.

I study how monetary policy set abroad reaches banks and firms at home. My dissertation examines how European Central Bank easing, including its negative-rate and unconventional phase, transmits through the CFA franc's hard euro peg to bank risk-taking, firms' access to credit, and corporate balance sheets in West and Central Africa.

My work sits at the intersection of international monetary economics, banking and corporate finance, using bank-level, firm-level and survey microdata. Alongside the PhD, I have advised on trade and climate finance policy in a pro bono capacity with GIZ.

Lead paper

External Monetary Passthrough under a Hard Peg: ECB Policy and Firm Finance in the CFA Zone

Working paperDraft available on request

How does ECB easing reach firms in an economy that has handed its monetary policy to Frankfurt? Firm-level survey evidence from the CFA franc zone shows that imported easing works mainly through firms' demand for credit and their perception of constraints rather than through wider formal access.

Abstract

Most evidence on global monetary spillovers comes from macro or bank-level data. I study the firm-level side of the transmission of ECB easing through the CFA franc zone's hard euro peg. Using harmonised World Bank Enterprise Survey microdata, a difference-in-differences design compares CFA firms with similar firms elsewhere in Sub-Saharan Africa before (2006–2011) and after (2014–2018) the ECB's easing cycle, complemented by CFA-only estimates on the ECB deposit facility rate. Imported easing is associated with a large rise in firms reporting no need for external credit, with unchanged application rates and a sharp fall in discouraged borrowers. Overdrafts expand faster than longer-horizon credit lines, and collateral requirements ease moderately. The results point to internal liquidity and latent credit rationing as the main channels in a shallow financial system, and to collateral registry, banking competition and supervisory reforms as conditions for turning better perceptions into lasting credit access.

Imported Easing and Bank Risk-Taking: ECB Policy, Credit Losses and Provisioning in CFA Zone Banks

Working paperDraft available on request

Does accommodative ECB policy encourage risk-taking by banks in the CFA zone, and do provisions and capital keep pace with the credit risk it creates?

Abstract

The risk-taking channel predicts that low policy rates push banks toward riskier borrowers and thinner buffers. I test whether this channel operates across a hard peg using audited accounts for 142 CFA zone banks from 2005 to 2024, linked to the ECB deposit facility rate. Easing is not associated with higher reported non-performing loan ratios, but it is associated with higher realised credit losses and higher impaired loans relative to equity. Net interest margins remain resilient under easing, which tempers the profit-squeeze motive for risk-taking. The paper also examines whether loan-loss provisioning and capital ratios adjust to the accumulated risk, with implications for regional prudential supervision under the BCEAO and BEAC.

Work in progress

Deploy or Hoard? ECB Easing and Firm Balance Sheets in the CFA Zone

Work in progress

When ECB rates fall, do CFA zone firms invest the liquidity they receive or hold it as a buffer? Firm accounts for 2007–2024 point to investment: fixed-asset ratios rise, liquid assets fall, and debt ratios and maturities increase with a one-year lag. Leverage responses concentrate in easing years and among riskier firms, which borrow more without investing more.
